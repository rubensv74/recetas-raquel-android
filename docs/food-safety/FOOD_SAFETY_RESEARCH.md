# FOOD SAFETY RESEARCH

## Executive conclusion

The research supports an evidence-centric, extensible architecture. It does not support generating a mass set of ingredient-allergen relations from general knowledge.

## Allergy vs intolerance

AESAN explicitly separates immune-mediated food allergy from food intolerance. The domain must do the same.

## Coeliac disease

Coeliac disease must remain distinct from wheat allergy and non-coeliac sensitivity. Regulation 828/2014 separately governs `gluten-free` and `very low gluten` claims.

## EU regulated groups

Annex II currently contains 14 groups. The consolidated text and Commission Notice preserve important details: exhaustive cereals/nuts lists, derivatives, compound-ingredient handling and explicit exemptions.

## Processing and derivatives

Processing cannot be translated into a universal risk-reduction rule. EFSA's scientific work and exemption procedure are case-specific. The 2024/2512 mustard amendment is a modern example of a narrowly defined exemption after targeted assessment.

## Cross-reactivity

EFSA includes cross-reactivity in scientific allergen evaluation, but cross-reactivity is not proof of presence. `POSSIBLE_CROSS_REACTIVITY` must remain distinct from `CONTAINS`.

## Cross-contact and PAL

Cross-contact is unintended presence. PAL is a precautionary statement, not direct ingredient declaration. Codex adopted a new risk-based PAL framework in July 2026; quantitative action-level logic remains outside catalog v1 pending final-text/applicability review.

## Allergens outside Annex II

The evidence base extends beyond the 14 EU groups. FAO/WHO Part 5 includes regional/national priority foods such as buckwheat and pine nut. WHO/IUIS provides broader protein nomenclature but explicitly does not establish clinical significance by inclusion alone.

## Compound/commercial ingredients

AESAN's February 2026 salsa-brava alert is directly relevant: possible allergens could not be determined because the components of a chicken-broth ingredient were unknown. This justifies a first-class `UNKNOWN / REQUIRES_REVIEW` state.

## Ingredient identity cannot prove absence of risk

AESAN's July 2026 alert found undeclared gluten in chickpea flour. Therefore the canonical identity `chickpea flour` cannot be interpreted as proof of `no gluten risk`; cross-contact/label context remains separate.

## Product doctrine

When evidence is incomplete, `INFORM THAT IT IS UNKNOWN` is preferred to `ASSUME IT IS SAFE`.
